Sellers often ask whether AI photos can replace their studio. The useful question is narrower: which of your images does each one do better?

## What each one is

A **studio shoot** books a photographer, a model, a set and a day. You bring the samples, and you leave with the images that were planned for that day.

An **AI catalog shoot** starts from product photos you take yourself. The studio reads the garment from those photos and produces six on-model frames on one consistent set.

## Where an AI catalog shoot is stronger

- **Volume.** Listing images for dozens of SKUs, where every product needs the same six views.
- **Consistency.** The same six frames in the same order for every product, so the catalog reads as one brand.
- **Speed to listing.** You can shoot a product the day the sample arrives.
- **A known price per product.** Shoots are bought in prepaid packs, so the price of one more SKU is known before you start. Current packs are in the [pricing section](/#pricing).
- **Reshoots.** If one frame is wrong, that one pose is generated again. Earlier versions are kept.

## Where a studio shoot is stronger

- **Campaigns.** A story, a location, several models interacting, a brand film.
- **Movement.** Fabric in motion and anything you want on video.
- **Unusual styling.** Layering, props and accessories that have to be exactly so.
- **Trust for a hero product.** For the few pieces that carry a launch, many brands want a physical shoot regardless.

## The risk to manage with AI

A generated image can look right and still show the wrong product. Guard against that in three ways:

- Give clear front and back photos, and a close-up of the detail that matters. [How to take product photos for an AI catalog shoot](/blog/product-photos-for-ai-catalog-shoot) covers this.
- Use a tool that checks each frame against your product photos and replaces a frame that fails.
- Compare the finished frames with the garment in your hand before you publish.

## How to compare the cost fairly

Ask your studio for a quote per finished product, not per day. Include the model, styling, retouching and the days your samples are away. Then compare that figure with the price of one SKU in the pack you would buy. Compare like with like: six finished listing frames per product on both sides.

## A simple way to split the work

- **Whole catalog:** AI catalog shoots, so every product has six consistent listing frames.
- **Hero pieces and campaigns:** a studio shoot, a few times a year.
- **New arrivals between campaigns:** AI catalog shoots, on the day the sample is ready.

The two are not rivals. One keeps the catalog complete and consistent; the other tells the brand's story.
