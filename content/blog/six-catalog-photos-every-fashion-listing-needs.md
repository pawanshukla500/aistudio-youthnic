A shopper cannot touch the garment, turn it over or hold it up to the light. The photos have to do that for them. Six frames cover the questions people actually ask before they buy clothing online.

Every shoot in Youthnic AI Studio delivers these six, in this order, so a whole catalog reads the same way from one product to the next.

## 1. Full front

Head to toe, from the front. This is the frame that appears in search results and category pages, so it has to show the complete outfit at a glance. In a planned shoot it is also the anchor: it fixes the model, the set and the light for the other five.

![Full front frame of a block-printed cotton top with black trousers](/images/showcase/kitkat-kattha-pose-1.webp)

## 2. Turning

A three-quarter turn. It shows what a flat front view hides: how the fabric drapes, how deep the side slit goes, where the seams sit.

## 3. Back

The back of a garment is where listings most often go wrong, because it is tempting to imagine it. The back frame should follow your own back photo: neck shape, ties and closures, seams, print placement and length. Nothing should be invented.

![Back frame of the same top, showing the lace-up closure](/images/showcase/kitkat-kattha-pose-3.webp)

## 4. Signature pose

One frame with character: seated, leaning, or in the posture of your style reference. It is the image people remember and the one most often used for banners and social posts. The garment still has to be exact; only the posture is borrowed.

## 5. Face and details

A closer frame with a natural face and a sharp view of the garment's signature work, such as yoke embroidery or neckline craft. It tells the shopper what the upper half looks like at conversation distance.

## 6. Product highlight

A tight close-up of the most distinctive feature, cropped where it sits: a cuff, a border, a back closure, a print. This is the frame that justifies the price.

![Close-up of the lace-up back closure on the cotton top](/images/showcase/kitkat-kattha-pose-6.webp)

## Why the order and the set matter

When every product page shows the same six views in the same order, shoppers learn where to look. They compare two kurtas by flicking to frame three on both.

The set matters for the same reason. If the wall, the floor and the light change between frames, the eye reads the change as a different product. Keep one set per shoot and change only the pose, the camera angle and the framing.

## A quick checklist before you publish

- The full front shows the whole outfit, including footwear.
- The back matches your back photo.
- The colour matches the garment in daylight.
- The close-up shows a real feature of this product, not a generic texture.
- All six frames share one model, one set and one light.
