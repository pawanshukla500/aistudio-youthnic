The finished frames can only be as accurate as the photos you start with. The studio takes the clothing from your product photos and from nowhere else, so anything those photos do not show has to be guessed, and a guess is where mistakes come from.

You do not need a camera or a studio. You need a phone, daylight and ten minutes per product.

## The two photos you must have

**Front** and **back**. Shoot both the same way:

- Show the whole garment, top to hem, with a little space around it.
- Hold the phone at chest height and keep it level, so the length is not distorted.
- Use daylight from a window, or shade outdoors. Avoid a yellow bulb and avoid flash.
- Keep the garment as it is worn: buttoned, tied, dupatta in place.

The garment can be on a person, a mannequin or a hanger. A person or mannequin is better, because it shows the true fall of the fabric.

![A back product photo of a kaftan worn over jeans, taken on a phone](/images/showcase/wine-kaftan-ref-back.webp)

## The photos that remove guesses

Each of these is optional, and each one makes a specific part of the result more reliable.

- **Fabric or embroidery close-up.** Fill the frame with the most detailed area. This is what the face and details frame and the product highlight are built from.
- **Full length.** If the front photo crops the hem, add one that shows it.
- **Sleeve or side.** Spread a sleeve or show the side slit when the shape is unusual.
- **Bottom wear.** For a set, photograph the bottom separately.

![A close product photo of an embroidered panel with mirror work](/images/showcase/wine-kaftan-ref-fabric.webp)

## What to avoid

- **Filters and beauty modes.** They shift colour, and the colour is read from your photo.
- **Heavy creases.** Steam the garment first; a crease can be read as a seam.
- **Busy backgrounds touching the garment.** A plain wall behind the product is enough.
- **Different lighting for front and back.** Shoot both in the same spot.
- **Cropped necklines or hems.** If it is not in the photo, it is a guess.

## Notes that help

If a detail matters and is hard to see, write it in the product notes: "lace-up back with tassels", "sleeves end at the elbow", "dupatta is sheer". Notes are read together with the photos.

## A style reference is not a product photo

A style reference is a picture of the kind of room, floor and lighting you want. The outfit in that picture is ignored. Use it to say "shoot it like this", never to show the product.

## Sarees need two more photos

A saree shoot needs four product photos instead of two. [Saree catalog photography with AI](/blog/saree-catalog-photography-with-ai) lists them.

## Before you upload

- Front and back, whole garment, same light.
- One sharp close-up of the best detail.
- No filters.
- Notes for anything the photos cannot show.

Then [start a shoot](/) and compare the six frames with the garment in your hand.
